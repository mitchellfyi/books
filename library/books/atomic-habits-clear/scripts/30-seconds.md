Atomic Habits turns behaviour change into design. Choose a tiny repeatable action, attach it to a visible cue, make it attractive and easy, then reward completion quickly. Reverse those rules for unwanted habits. Its best insight is that systems and environments often beat willpower; each repetition can also support a chosen identity. The method is practical and research-aligned, but one-percent compounding is a metaphor, identity evidence is mixed, and clinical or structural problems need more than personal routines.
