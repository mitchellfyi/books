Meditations is the private notebook of a Roman emperor: Marcus Aurelius, writing to himself at night during plague and war, never intending readers. The exercises — judge the judgment, not the event; control what's yours; view your grievances from above; keep death visible — are the originals of everything modern thought-distancing resells. Scholars agree it's not original philosophy. It's something rarer: the practice itself, tested at maximum load, trusted for eighteen centuries.
