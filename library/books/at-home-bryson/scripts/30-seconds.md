At Home, by Bill Bryson, tours his Victorian rectory room by room and lets each one open onto history: the kitchen onto the spice trade, the bedroom onto sex and death, the stairs onto a hundred ways to fall. Wildly digressive, endlessly quotable, and pure pleasure for lovers of everyday history. Expect a cabinet of curiosities, not an argument — one historian said it occasionally has better jokes than a sense of history.
