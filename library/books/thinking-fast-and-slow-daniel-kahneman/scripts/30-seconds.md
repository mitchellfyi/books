Thinking, Fast and Slow explains how intuition produces quick, convincing judgments, while effortful reasoning only sometimes checks them. Kahneman shows why we neglect base rates, trust coherent stories, misread luck, underestimate projects and frame gains differently from losses. The best corrections are practical: use reference classes, structured rules and wider frames. It is an excellent decision-making map, though some famous priming evidence failed to age well, and its two systems are useful metaphors, not literal brain machinery.
