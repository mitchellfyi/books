The Farther Reaches of Human Nature is Abraham Maslow's posthumous capstone: late essays on what people are like at their best, self-actualization as daily choices, peak experiences, the hunger for meaning, and the transcendence he placed above his famous pyramid. Visionary, warm, and historically huge, it suits readers tracing psychology's growth tradition to its source. Expect inspiration and vocabulary, not evidence: the science was, by his own admission, a promissory note.
