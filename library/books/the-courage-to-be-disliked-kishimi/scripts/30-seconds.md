The Courage to Be Disliked, by Ichiro Kishimi and Fumitake Koga, argues that freedom begins when your past and other people's approval stop making your choices. Its best tool is separation of tasks: own your actions, but not another person's judgment. Pair that boundary with contribution, not isolation. The book is memorable and useful, but its denial of trauma is false and potentially harmful. Read it for agency and relationships, never as clinical guidance. Content rating: 5.7 out of 10.
