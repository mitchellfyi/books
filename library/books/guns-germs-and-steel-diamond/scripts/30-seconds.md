Jared Diamond asks why Eurasian societies gained guns, steel, writing, states and epidemic diseases before societies on other continents. His answer is geography, not human ability: Eurasia had more useful wild species and easier routes for spreading farming. Food surpluses then supported specialists, technology and armies, while livestock bred lethal diseases. It is a powerful account of continental prehistory, but it underweights culture, institutions and political choice. Content rating: six point four out of ten.
