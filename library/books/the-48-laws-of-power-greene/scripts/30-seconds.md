The 48 Laws of Power, by Robert Greene, distils three thousand years of court intrigue and statecraft into forty-eight deliberately amoral rules for winning influence: guard your reputation, conceal your intentions, never outshine the master. It suits people in openly political environments, and anyone wanting manipulation named so they can resist it. Be warned: the history is cherry-picked, the laws contradict each other, and applied literally in a trusting workplace they backfire badly.
